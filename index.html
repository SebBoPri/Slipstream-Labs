The problem

Building has never been easier. Knowing what to build has never mattered more.

The biggest risk in any product is value risk: whether people will actually use it or pay for it. It is the risk that kills products, and the one most often answered with opinion.

  • 01 A product everyone agreed on, built from what the team believed customers wanted. It shipped. Nobody used it.
  • 02 Customers asked for a feature. It shipped. It solved the request, not the problem.
  • 03 "Do something with AI" became the goal. It shipped. There was no problem it solved.
Flagship engagement

The Discovery Sprint.

From assumption to decision, supported by evidence.
Know whether the idea holds before you spend significant time and resources finding out. We set the bar for proof, gather the evidence together, and make the call while it is still cheap to be wrong.

  1. 01

    Sharpen

    A working session with the people who own the decision. What are we trying to decide? Who has the problem? What would have to be true for it to work? Which assumption is most dangerous? We leave with that assumption named and the kill-lines agreed: what evidence would make you stop, set before any data arrives.

  2. 02

    Test

    Slipstream Labs designs the guide, sequences the outreach, and debriefs regularly to keep the read honest. We focus on what customers actually do: their behaviour, their workflows, and what they have already tried, not whether they like the idea.

  3. 03

    Decide

    A working session where the evidence meets the kill-lines. What we know. What remains uncertain. Which assumptions survived, which failed, and what that means: proceed, reframe, or stop. The decision memo records the call so the team does not relitigate it.

Approach

Start with what must be true.

Product teams rarely lack ideas. They lack certainty about which assumptions matter. We start with the decision, then work backwards. What problem are we solving? Who has it? How are they dealing with it today? What would have to be true for this to work? Which assumption is most dangerous? Then we test.

Just because you can build it does not mean you should. Speed is only an advantage when you have the right direction.

Field notes

A team has a roadmap. Nobody is confident which part of it is right.

What a discovery sprint changes

A discovery sprint tests which problem is real and who has it, then gives the team the confidence to decide what to do next.

What a strategy sprint changes

A strategy sprint weighs the directions on evidence, not opinion, and gives the team the confidence to commit.

Everyone agrees the team should do something with AI. Nobody can say what, or why it would matter to customers.

What an AI sprint changes

An AI sprint ranks use cases on real value and feasibility, then scopes a first experiment small enough to start soon.
